Spatial Computing Lab Abstract

LUCA School of Arts (LUCA)
Partner: FilmEU
Coordinator: Jan Duerinck
Contact: jan.duerinck@luca-arts.be
Website: https://www.researchcatalogue.net/

Focus: Augmented/mixed reality, spatial computing, and interactive media

Primary Goals: Advance interdisciplinary research in XR and spatial computing; strengthen doctoral and practice-based research.

Lab Description: The Spatial Computing Lab (LUCAscl*) at LUCA School of Arts explores real-time spatial interaction, augmented and mixed reality, and immersive sound. Integrating panoramic video, depth sensors, and tactile interfaces, it fosters artistic research into the dynamic relations between digital and physical space. The lab aims to develop critical, experimental, and participatory approaches to spatial computing and media art. As part of WIRE, it contributes to interdisciplinary research across XR, installation practices, and interactive design. Its goals include strengthening collaborations, expanding its technological capabilities, and building a sustainable framework that supports doctoral and practice-based research within LUCA and FilmEU.

Abstract VLAIO

Abstract: VLAIO, Situated Media Lab

Framework: VLAIO, Onderzoeksinfrastructuur Hogescholen (Research Infrastructure for Universities of Applied Sciences) · Applicant: LUCA School of Arts, through the R&I Lab Data, AI & Creativity · Status: submitted September 2026, decision pending

New media formats (immersive, interactive, AI-driven, and responsive to place, performers and audience) have no shared methods for developing and validating them across changing contexts. In 2026, LUCA applied to VLAIO for a Situated Media Lab. This is a shared research infrastructure that treats context itself as a research variable: configurations can be built, tested, moved and tested again in different spaces.

The Situated Media Lab builds on the Spatial Computing Lab and does not replace it. It adds three layers:

  • a shared compute, data and integration layer that connects existing LUCA labs
  • a fixed research node at campus Lemmens, linked to the SCL for synchronised audio, video and sensing capture
  • a mobile, edge-first node for theatres, museums and partner venues

Three reference cases test the infrastructure:

  • portable immersive formats: content that moves between very different spaces
  • embodied AI: sensing and AI combined into responsive physical–digital environments
  • connected live performance: real-time exchange between separate performance, control and immersive spaces, with the SCL as one of the linked spaces

For the Spatial Computing Lab, the application means moving from a single immersive room to one node in a reconfigurable network. Its custom components, tracking and spatial audio become reusable building blocks for comparative research across contexts.

Abstract FilmEU

Abstract: FilmEU

Framework: FilmEU, the European University for Film and Media Arts (FilmEU+, Erasmus+ European Universities) · Related research project: WIRE

FilmEU is an alliance of European higher-education institutions in film and media arts. Its Imagination Centre connects the partners' local labs, each with its own expertise:

  • virtual production and motion capture
  • immersive sound and Dolby Atmos
  • digital fabrication
  • generative AI

The Spatial Computing Lab is LUCA's contribution, and one of the first labs in the network that is fully operating. It brings a rare combination to the network: immersive projection on walls and floor, a 16-speaker third-order ambisonic sound system, and real-time body tracking in one room.

Within FilmEU the lab has two roles:

  • education: students from across the alliance work in it through coursework, electives and joint programmes, such as the planned BA Music and Sound in Immersive Multimedia Environments
  • artistic research: through the WIRE project, visiting researchers develop and present work in the lab

In June 2026, at the FilmEU Summit in Brussels, the lab hosted two WIRE pilot showcases:

  • Agnes Meng (Lusófona University): Por Este Rio Acima, an immersive documentary installation on Northern Portuguese folklore
  • Diego Barajas (Tallinn University): Eclosion, an interactive immersive experience

These visits shaped one of the lab's central principles: preparation should happen remotely. The documented components and simulation tools let visitors arrive ready to spend their time on site on the work that needs the room. FilmEU is also developing shared access and governance models, such as a token-based system for booking labs across partners.

Abstract STAGEtech

Abstract: StageTech Flanders

Partners: LUCA School of Arts, Corda Campus, imec · Format: artistic residency through an open call

Technology doesn't make art. Artists do. StageTech Flanders is a long-term platform where artists, researchers and technologists explore emerging audiovisual technologies through artistic research. These include AI, spatial computing, virtual production, immersive sound and real-time systems. In StageTech, artistic questions lead and production comes after experimentation.

The first open call (deadline 15 September 2026) supports a residency from October 2026. Selected artists receive:

  • a project budget of €30,000
  • access to research and production facilities
  • technical and artistic support from the three partners

The trajectory may include workshops or experimental sessions with LUCA students. It ends in a public presentation at the Corda Arena, planned for April 2027.

For the Spatial Computing Lab, StageTech is a way for its research to reach professional artistic practice. The lab offers a space where ideas for immersive installations and performances can be tried in a controlled setting with tracking, projection and spatial sound before they move to a larger stage. Its documented, reusable components help resident artists reach a working prototype quickly. StageTech, in turn, brings external artistic questions back into the lab's research and teaching.

Lab tools - Introduction

Lab tools: an introduction

Abstract

The Spatial Computing Lab (LUCAscl*) gives artists, students and researchers a shared set of custom TouchDesigner components ("toxes"). These components hide the lab's specific hardware behind a small number of stable interfaces. There are eight components, grouped into three layers:

  • Sensing: body tracking and camera images from Azure Kinect sensors.
  • Spatial logic: virtual objects, and calculations between bodies and objects.
  • Output: wall projection, floor projection and third-order ambisonic sound.

Together they let a user go from a tracked body in the room to an image or sound that responds to it, without rebuilding the lab's signal chain. The components are meant to be used both at home and in the lab. Projects can be prepared and simulated remotely, and then run at full resolution and with live sensor data on site. On the sound side, the components are complemented by a rendering patch in Max/MSP. This note describes the system the components belong to, the conventions they share and how the other notes in this collection fit together.

Azure Kinect sensor

Sensor: Azure Kinect skeleton

Component: Azure_Kinect_sensor.tox · Layer: sensing · In: none · Out: one CHOP per selected joint (tx ty tz, metres)

Abstract

This component gives live three-dimensional positions of human body joints, measured by the lab's Azure Kinect sensors. You choose which joints you need, and each one gets its own outlet as a CHOP with the position in room coordinates. When you are away from the lab, a mouse mode stands in for the tracked body. This lets you develop and test interaction logic without sensors, and the patch keeps the same structure in the lab.

Role in the lab

The component is the main way bodies enter the system. Its outputs feed:

  • Calculations (CALC), to relate a body to a virtual object
  • Virtual object viewer, to check the tracking
  • Ambisonics sound control, to place sound sources on a moving body

It covers position only. For images from the same sensors, use Azure Kinect camera.

Technical description

  • Sensor. Azure Kinect devices combine a time-of-flight depth camera with an RGB camera. Skeleton estimation is done by the Azure Kinect Body Tracking pipeline, which gives a fixed set of named joints per person. The component exposes a chosen subset (for example head, hand_l, hand_r, pelvis).
  • Mounting. The three sensors hang on drop rods below the pipe grid under the ceiling, at roughly the height of the top of the walls. They look down into the room from three sides: above the west half, above the east side, and above the entrance. Mounting them high reduces the chance of one person hiding another and keeps the floor free. See Lab 3D model for their approximate positions.
  • Coordinate frame. Output values are in metres in the lab's room frame: X = width, Y = height, Z = depth. This is the same frame used by Virtual object and the sound system, so you can compare joint and object positions directly without converting.

Azure Kinect camera

Camera: Azure Kinect

Component: Azure_Kinect_camera.tox · Layer: sensing · In: none · Out: one TOP per sensor

Abstract

This component gives the live image streams of the lab's three Azure Kinect sensors as TouchDesigner TOPs, one per sensor. It is for work that uses the camera image itself, such as live video in a projection, image analysis, or material for compositing. Skeleton data is not included: for body positions, use Azure Kinect sensor.

Role in the lab

The camera and the sensor component use the same hardware, but they keep image and position data apart. The camera's TOPs normally go to image processing and then to Wall projection or Floor projection.

Technical description

  • Source. The video comes from a separate capture PC, not from the TouchDesigner workstation itself. This spreads the processing load: the high-bandwidth camera streams do not compete with rendering on the workstation.
  • Viewpoint. The sensors are mounted on the ceiling grid, so all images are high-angle views looking down into the room.
  • Stream selection. Image sets which stream is delivered (for example colour). Other stream types the sensor provides, such as depth or infrared, can be selected here if the capture side publishes them.
  • Outlets. There is one outlet per sensor in a fixed order (1, 2, 3), which matches the physical sensors. To find out which outlet is which corner, wave at a camera.
  • Resolution and frame rate are set by the sensor and the stream configuration, not by the component. Fit or resample downstream to match the target resolution of a projection component.

Virtual object

Virtual object

Component: virtual_object.tox · Layer: spatial logic · In: position (CHOP, optional) · Out: shape (POP, blue), position + size (CHOP, green)

Abstract

A virtual object is an invisible geometric volume (a sphere, box or cylinder) that you place in the physical room for other components to interact with. It does not draw anything itself. It is a spatial definition that other components can measure against, listen to or display. It is the basic building block of the lab's interaction logic: zones, triggers, attractors and sound sources are all made from virtual objects.

Role in the lab

Virtual objects connect the tracked body to the content. A tracked hand has no meaning until it can be related to something. The virtual object provides that "something" in the shared room frame. Its shape output feeds Calculations (CALC) and the Virtual object viewer. Its position output can place a sound source in Ambisonics sound control.

Technical description

  • Primitives. You can choose a sphere (radius), a box (width X, height Y, depth Z) or a cylinder. Only the size fields that belong to the current shape are active.
  • Transform. Position XYZ and Rotate XYZ place the object in the room frame (metres; X = width, Y = height, Z = depth). If you wire a CHOP into the optional input, it drives the position, so an object can follow a tracked joint or an animated path.
  • Soft boundary. For spheres, Outer shell adds a second, larger radius around the core. The space between the two radii is a transition zone that CALC uses for its Fade to sphere calculation.
  • GPU geometry. Since the October 2026 revision, the shape is a POP (point operator), so geometry is processed on the GPU. Scenes with many objects stay responsive. Each object gets its own colour, which the viewer picks up automatically.

Virtual object viewer

Virtual object viewer

Component: virtual_object_viewer.tox · Layer: spatial logic (verification) · In: positions (CHOP), shapes (POP) · Out: 3D preview

Abstract

The viewer shows tracked points and virtual objects together in one 3D scene of the lab. Its only job is to check that everything is where you expect it to be. It does not change any data. The physical room is otherwise blind to what the system "thinks", and the viewer makes that state visible. Most spatial interaction design depends on this view for debugging and for explaining a setup.

Role in the lab

Interaction logic in the lab is invisible: virtual objects have no physical form, and tracking errors cannot be seen in the room. The viewer is where the sensor layer and the spatial-logic layer can be checked together before content is connected to the outputs.

Technical description

  • Scene. A wireframe box shows the physical room. Inside it, every tracked point wired into the green inputs is drawn as a sphere, and every Virtual object in the project is drawn in its own colour.
  • Automatic discovery. Virtual objects in the project are found automatically. They do not need to be wired one by one.
  • Viewports. The perspective view gives a quick overall check. The top view works as a live floor plan, which is useful for placing zones relative to the entrance and the projection surfaces.
  • Read-only. The component has no side effects. You can add or remove it at any time without changing a patch's behaviour.

Calculations (CALC)

Calculations (CALC)

Component: CALC.tox · Layer: spatial logic · In: 2 POPs (point or shape) · Out: 1 CHOP

Abstract

CALC measures how two spatial entities relate to each other: two points, a point and a shape, or two shapes. It reduces that relation to one control signal. Four measurements are available: centre-to-centre distance, signed distance to a surface, an inside/outside test, and a smooth fade between two concentric spheres. Together these are enough for most body-to-object interactions in the lab, such as proximity, entering a zone, and gradual transitions. They come from a single component with one predictable output.

Role in the lab

CALC turns geometry into control data. A typical setup has a tracked joint from Azure Kinect sensor on input 1 and a Virtual object on input 2. The resulting CHOP drives any parameter: the opacity of a projected layer, the gain of a sound, the state of a scene.

Technical description

Let A be input 1 and B be input 2.

  • Distance between points. The Euclidean distance between the centres of A and B:
    center_dist = ‖c_A − c_B‖ (metres).
  • Distance between shapes. The distance from A to the surface of B, or between the surfaces of A and B. The result is signed: positive outside, negative inside. This works for every closed shape. For boxes and cylinders, the distance is measured outward from the shape's centre, so values near edges and corners are slightly larger than the true Euclidean surface distance.
  • In or outside of a shape. A binary containment test: inside = 1 if A is inside B, otherwise 0.
  • Fade to sphere. A continuous value between the inner sphere (radius r_in) and the outer shell (radius r_out) of a sphere-type virtual object. It is 1 inside r_in, 0 beyond r_out, and changes smoothly in between. This makes a soft, non-binary interaction zone. It requires B to be a sphere with Outer shell turned on.

Ambisonics sound control

Ambisonics sound control

Component: Ambisonics_sound_ctrl.tox · Layer: output (sound) · In: one position per source (CHOP) + audio (CHOP) · Out: third-order ambisonic mix on the lab's 16 + 1 loudspeaker array

Abstract

This component places sound sources in the three-dimensional space of the lab. Each source has its own position, size and audio input, and the component renders them through the lab's third-order ambisonic loudspeaker system. The user works only in TouchDesigner: wire in a position (for example a Virtual object or a tracked joint), point to an audio signal, and set a range. Encoding, decoding to the irregular speaker layout and transport over the Dante network are handled by a dedicated rendering chain in Max/MSP. This gives everyday users of the lab position-driven spatial sound, which would otherwise require specialist knowledge of ambisonics.

Role in the lab

This is the sound counterpart of Wall projection and Floor projection. It is where the spatial logic of a project becomes audible. A virtual object wired into a source makes that object heard at its location. A tracked joint makes a sound follow a body.

Technical description

Signal chain

  1. TouchDesigner (HP Z8 workstation). The component holds one source per virtual object slot. Each source has a position (CHOP inlet), an audio input (a CHOP or channel) and a Rangegain. Audio is resampled automatically to the Dante network rate of 48 kHz. The internal network is built and wired by script, which re-checks the structure about once per second.
  2. Transport. Source positions are sent over OSC. Source audio is sent over Dante (Dante Via on the workstation, Dante Virtual Soundcard in ASIO mode on the rendering machine).
  3. Rendering (Windows NUC, Max/MSP). Described in full in Max-MSP ambisonic renderer.
  • Each position is converted to azimuth, elevation and distance relative to a listening reference point.

Wall projection

Wall projection

Component: wall_projection.tox · Layer: output (image) · In: 1–3 TOPs · Out: projection window + resolution CHOP (resX, resY)

Abstract

This component is the output stage for the lab's wall projectors. It opens the projection window, sends the images to the wall surfaces, and tells the user the exact resolution the source images must have, both at home and in the lab. The resolution comes from the component as a CHOP that the user wires into their own source. This keeps content and output in sync by design, so there is no stretching, cropping or black bars.

Role in the lab

Together with Floor projection and Ambisonics sound control, this component forms the lab's output layer. Any image from a project, whether generated, filmed (Azure Kinect camera) or controlled by interaction logic, reaches the walls through this component.

Technical description

  • Projection modes.
  • Three screens (default, both switches off): three separate images on the left, centre and right walls (inputs 1, 2 and 3).
  • Panoramic projection: one wide image across the whole wall surface from input 1. Inputs 2 and 3 are ignored.
  • Single projection: output to one projector only, for tests and small setups.
  • Wall layout. The three channels correspond to the three fixed walls: left, centre and right as seen from the entrance (west, north and east). The fourth (south) wall is mobile and is not installed by default. When a project uses it, its output is set up for that project and is not part of this component's standard configuration.
  • Resolution contract. The resolution CHOP (resX, resY) always gives the resolution for the current mode and licence. Users wire it into their source TOP instead of typing numbers. If the mode or licence changes, the content follows automatically.

Floor projection

Floor projection

Component: floor_projection.tox · Layer: output (image) · In: 1 TOP · Out: projection window + resolution CHOP (resX, resY)

Abstract

This component is the output stage for the lab's floor projector. It works the same way as Wall projection, but for a roughly square surface with no natural "up". The floor matters in spatial computing because it is where tracked bodies, virtual objects and projected images line up most directly. A position in the room's XZ plane corresponds to a point on the floor image.

Role in the lab

The floor projection completes the lab's image output. It is especially suited to interaction based on location: zones, paths and traces that respond to where visitors stand. These are typically made by combining Azure Kinect sensor data with Virtual object zones and Calculations (CALC).

Technical description

  • Resolution contract. As with the wall projection, the component publishes resX, resY as a CHOP that you wire into your source TOP.
  • Recommended chain: moviefilein → fit → floor_projection. A Fit TOP before the component makes sure that content made for other aspect ratios (for example wide wall content) is fitted on purpose and not distorted.
  • Home/lab switch. License off = reduced home resolution (within the 1280 × 1280 limit of the Non-Commercial licence). On = full lab resolution (Educational licence on the lab workstation).
  • Orientation. The floor has no inherent up. By convention, orient the content towards where the audience enters (the south side, where the entrance is) and write the orientation down in the project documentation.
  • Mapping to room coordinates. Because the room frame uses X = width and Z = depth, positions from the sensor and from virtual objects can be mapped to floor pixel coordinates with a linear scale to resX, resY. This mapping is the user's responsibility, as the component does not apply it.

Max-MSP ambisonic renderer

Max/MSP ambisonic renderer

Patch: Ambix_3th_order-sound_ctrl.maxpat · Host: Windows NUC, Max 9.1.5 · Layer: output (sound, back end) · In: OSC positions (UDP 10000) + 16 audio channels over Dante · Out: 16 loudspeaker feeds + 1 subwoofer feed over Dante

Abstract

This patch is the rendering engine behind Ambisonics sound control. It receives the positions of up to sixteen sound sources and their audio, encodes them into a third-order ambisonic sound field, and decodes that field to the lab's measured, irregular layout of sixteen loudspeakers plus a subwoofer, all in real time. Most users never touch it: the TouchDesigner component is the interface, and the renderer runs on a dedicated machine. Splitting the work this way keeps audio rendering apart from the graphics and tracking load on the main workstation. It also keeps the decoder calibration in one place, independent of any project.

Role in the lab

The renderer is the second half of the lab's spatial sound chain. Ambisonics sound control decides where each sound is and what it plays. The renderer turns that into speaker signals for this particular room. Because the calibration lives here, any TouchDesigner project that sends positions and audio in the expected format sounds correct in the lab without project-specific tuning.

Lab 3D model

Lab 3D model

Abstract

The 3D model is an interactive spatial reference for the Spatial Computing Lab. It shows the three fixed projection walls, the floor projection, the mobile fourth wall, the ceiling grid, the sixteen loudspeakers, the Azure Kinect sensors and the reference listening point, all in the room coordinate frame used by every lab component. It is meant to answer where is what? before a visit: to plan where zones and virtual objects go relative to the walls and the entrance, to understand the speaker layout behind Max-MSP ambisonic renderer, and to compare positions in TouchDesigner with the physical room.
