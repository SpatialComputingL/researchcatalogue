Full text

Lab Description

Initiated in 2020 by the Mediated Environments research cluster, the Spatial Computing Lab* (*SCL) at LUCA School of Arts, Campus Sint-Lukas Brussels, serves as an artistic and technological research space, multimedia workshop, and presentation venue. It integrates immersive video projection, depth sensors, and spatial sound to explore new forms of interaction and experience.

Goals

The lab focuses on real-time spatial analytics to redefine interactions between digital and physical spaces. With an emphasis on augmented and mixed reality, it shifts away from traditional virtual reality and production but remains open to fluid boundaries between these domains. By providing essential tools to traverse virtual and physical environments, the lab seeks to extend its explorations into both immersive digital experiences and site-specific installations.

State of the art

Position tracking, body & image recognition, [immersive] projection mapping, point cloud data generation, live ray tracing, volumetric sound engineering… are among the applications aiming to push the boundaries of what's possible in and outside the *SCL.

Working with state-of-the-art technologies in the lab allows to critically engage with tools that shape society, often in unseen ways. By integrating position tracking, image recognition, projection mapping, and sound engineering into artistic and research contexts, we examine their impact beyond commercial and scientific applications. This interdisciplinary approach fosters new perspectives, challenging conventions and uncovering alternative insights. Through experimentation, we push the boundaries of perception and knowledge, contributing to a deeper understanding of technology's role in contemporary society.

The Spatial Computing Lab is at a pivotal stage in defining its research trajectory. Discussions with key stakeholders highlight the need for a structured research framework that integrates the lab's capabilities into broader institutional and (inter-)national initiatives.

As part of this effort, an inventory of the lab's current state ("as is") is being compiled, along with a vision statement outlining its potential role in enhancing research and innovation (R&I). The lab's position within the WIRE framework and its links to virtual production ecosystems are key considerations. Additionally, mapping its connections with existing LUCA research clusters and interdisciplinary collaborations will strengthen its strategic alignment.

The next steps involve finalizing the SCL* vision statement, identifying relevant funding calls (including WIRE 2026), and positioning the lab within LUCA's wider network of workspaces. This structured approach aims to establish SCL* as a recognized research hub, ensuring long-term sustainability and impact.

Implementation

On a technical level, the Spatial Computing Lab, since its founding, has already made significant strides in expanding its technological capabilities. The audio setup has been upgraded to an octophone installation, with soundproofing the space in regards. The panoramic video system has improved in projection quality. More importantly, the integration of RGB cameras has paved the way for exploring and implementing various depth sensors, enabling more accurate position tracking and overall creative output.The next steps aim to enhance the lab's capabilities by upgrading and expanding the installation into an even more immersive experience. (More detailed description below in the budget section.)

On a substantive level, SCL* is benefitting from its connection to various departments at LUCA School of Arts to grow, including media and information design, fine arts, audiovisual arts, film, and architecture. Research and experimentation are central to its approach. Master and bachelor electives—such as 'Human computer interaction'—foster interdisciplinary collaboration, allowing students to engage with technology from diverse perspectives as creators with varying research expectations.

The lab is enabling more support provided to students through custom modules that accelerate their learning curve and expand their time for experimentation. While researchers focus on in-depth exploration of specific use cases and techniques, in turn providing useful tools or insights for further exploration in the lab.

Budget FilmEU / Expansion

The presented budget provides a detailed overview of the investment required to advance the Spatial Computing Lab at LUCA School of Arts, Campus Brussel Sint-Lukas, in alignment with the FilmEU integration. It aims to enhance the lab's technological capabilities by upgrading the audiovisual setup, expanding the panoramic video installation into a more immersive experience. All taking place in a newly sourced location, doubling its current size. Designed for longevity and flexibility, the setup will be modular and adaptable to space and time constraints.

The budget includes spatial modifications, an overhead grid system, sound- and light-proofing curtains, projection screens, an expanded workstation, and additional hardware, such as video capture for documentation. It covers the minimum requirements for replacing the existing audiovisual setup and completing all projection surfaces, including wall and floor projections. These improvements will maximize the functionality of the grid system and workspace, increase user capacity while also establishing the lab as a platform for presentations and hands-on art practice courses.
